Singaporeans love to complain that the country is boring, but I don't think it is.

Growing up in Atlanta (and then going to college there), I found myself feeling the same way. I hung out with the same people, went to the same places, did the same things, and complained about how boring it was and how there was nothing to do. It wasn't until my last semester of college, when it finally hit me that I had to leave, that I actually started to look around me and appreciate the place I called home. I started exploring parts of the city that I didn't know existed, trying restaurants that I had been hearing about but never thought about actually going to, and doing all the activities I had always said I wanted to do. When you spend a lot of time in a place, you feel like there will always be more time, so you keep pushing things off until "later." But at some point in everyone's life, they realize that time has run out, that later has turned into never, and that the opportunities they had will never come back.

So when I moved to Singapore, I made it a point not to think like that. I made a list of all the places I wanted to explore, the food I wanted to eat, and the things I wanted to do. But when I tried to find people to do them with, most people just weren't interested. Instead, they said that it was all lame and that Singapore was boring. I understand where they're coming from, but I disagree. No matter where you are in the world, if you're there long enough, it will start to feel boring. It is up to you to make your life interesting.

The only thing that makes a place interesting or boring, then, is the existence of opportunities, or the lack thereof. So I made a list of all the different activities and hobbies someone could have, and then set out to see which cities are truly interesting or boring.
