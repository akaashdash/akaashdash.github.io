Singaporeans love to complain that the country is boring, but I don't think it is.

Growing up in Atlanta (and then going to college there), I found myself feeling the same way. I hung out with the same people, went to the same places, did the same things, and complained about how boring it was and how there was nothing to do. It wasn't until my last semester of college, when it finally hit me that I had to leave, that I actually started to look around me and appreciate the place I called home. I started exploring parts of the city that I didn't know existed, trying restaurants that I had been hearing about but never thought about actually going to, and doing all the activities I had always said I wanted to do. When you spend a lot of time in a place, you feel like there will always be more time, so you keep pushing things off until "later." But at some point in everyone's life, they realize that time has run out, that later has turned into never, and that the opportunities they had will never come back.

So when I moved to Singapore, I made it a point not to think like that. I made a list of all the places I wanted to explore, the food I wanted to eat, and the things I wanted to do. But when I tried to find people to do them with, most people just weren't interested. Instead, they said that it was all lame and that Singapore was boring. I understand where they're coming from, but I disagree. No matter where you are in the world, if you're there long enough, it will start to feel boring. It is up to you to make your life interesting.

The only thing that makes a place interesting or boring, then, is the existence of opportunities, or the lack thereof. So I made a list of all the different activities and hobbies someone could have, and then set out to see which cities are truly interesting or boring.

## singapore first

Of 299 activities, **227 (76%)** can be done in Singapore as the real thing, and another 38 in a limited form (indoor skiing, simulators, one-off workshops). That's 89% in some form. Only 34 can't be done at all.

Most of the list came from my own head, which would flatter the result. So I also counted just the 156 activities that came from independent Wikipedia lists of sports, games, crafts, dance and collectables. For those, **68%** are fully doable and **84%** at least partly. The honest number is closer to that one.

The shape matters more than the percentage:

- **Where Singapore is rich.** Every food and drink activity, every performing art and every game or mind sport on the list had a taker. So did almost all crafts, from pottery to glassblowing to perfume-making.
- **Where it's thin.** Anything that needs terrain or weather: snow, caves, rapids, mountains, deserts, hunting. In the extreme and exotic category only 10 of 25 were doable, and several of those were simulations.
- **It's close and cheap.** About 70% of the doable activities are within 15 km of Raffles Place and cost under S$60 a session. 248 of 265 welcome a complete beginner.

## and twelve other cities

I ran the same list through Hong Kong, Tokyo, London, New York, Atlanta, Sydney, San Francisco, Paris, Chicago, Dubai, Shanghai and Beijing. This is where I have to be careful, because the research isn't equal. Singapore has all 299 activities checked. London has 287, Hong Kong 277, New York 276, Paris 276, Atlanta 268, Sydney 267, San Francisco 267, Tokyo 262, Chicago 259, Dubai 250, Shanghai 221 and Beijing 204. An activity with no result means I couldn't find evidence, which is not the same as "can't be done", and the missing ones tend to be the niche ones. So comparing each city's raw percentage would be unfair. The page only compares activities that have a result in every city you pick.

On the **196 activities with a result in all of the ten best-covered cities**:

| | fully doable | at least partly |
|---|---|---|
| Singapore | 84% | 90% |
| London | 83% | 90% |
| Hong Kong | 80% | 92% |
| New York | 88% | 91% |
| Paris | 83% | 90% |
| Atlanta | 78% | 89% |
| San Francisco | 80% | 92% |
| Sydney | 77% | 91% |
| Tokyo | 77% | 90% |
| Chicago | 81% | 91% |

That's the whole story: a few points either way is noise, and nobody is meaningfully less boring than anybody else. Singapore sits in the middle. The gaps are in the same place everywhere: sports are the weakest broad category at 62 to 73% (the long tail of obscure ones like curling), and the extreme end (snow, caves, canyoning, BASE jumping) is mostly out of reach in every city (9 to 27%), because it depends on geography. "At least partly" includes things like a day trip to a ski hill or drop zone more than 30 km out, so it flatters every city a little. What differs is mostly geography too: ocean surfing exists in Hong Kong, New York, San Francisco and Sydney but not in Singapore, London, Atlanta, Tokyo, Paris or Chicago. About 44% of the 196 differ in at least one city, but most of those look like holes in my research rather than real absences. There is no reason Tokyo lacks watercolour painting.

Cost and distance look alike as well. Between 61% and 84% of the doable activities in each city are within 15 km of the centre and cheap enough to try on a whim (under about US$45).

Dubai, Shanghai and Beijing are in the explorer too, but I haven't researched them far enough to put in the table. Add them to the comparison and the shared set of activities shrinks, so read those percentages loosely.

## what this doesn't show

- It's not a share of all human hobbies. The list is a sample, seeded from Wikipedia lists plus my own ideas, and I built it while researching Singapore first, so it probably under-counts things only other cities have.
- "Doable" means a venue, club or class exists within about 30 km of the city centre. It doesn't mean it's good, convenient or uncrowded. Cost and distance are rough tiers.
- Research hit a web-search limit repeatedly, so a lot of rows rest on a single listing or aggregator page. Between just under a fifth (New York) and nearly half (Beijing) of each city's rows are low-confidence. Only about 41% (Beijing) to 75% (New York) of each city's rows clear a stricter bar: not low-confidence, a source link that still works, and a page that isn't clearly about something else.
- English-language search and listings find some cities far more easily than others. Beijing and Shanghai in particular are underrepresented because the sources I could reach were thin and dated, so their numbers say more about my search than about those cities.
- "Searched, found nothing" is weaker than it sounds, and a few activities (Peranakan beadwork, Malay dance) are specific to Singapore and weren't searched elsewhere.
- The venue haze on the map is OpenStreetMap, whose completeness differs by city. New York has roughly nine times as many tagged sports facilities as London, which says more about the mapping than the city. Only about half the cities have venue data at all.
